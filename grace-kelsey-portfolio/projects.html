Green Space Access in Genesee County

Mapping the relationship between household income, pedestrian infrastructure, and access to parks.

Access to quality green space can support public health by reducing stress, encouraging active lifestyles, and improving quality of life. Yet that access is not always distributed fairly. In this spatial analysis project, I’m examining how household income, pedestrian infrastructure, and park access intersect across Genesee County.

Using ArcGIS Pro, I layered U.S. Census demographic data with local park boundaries, sidewalk networks, and bus routes to map walking-distance buffers. The analysis highlighted disparities: lower-income neighborhoods may face barriers such as major roads with few pedestrian crossings or limited transit options to reach community green spaces.

The project is ongoing. I plan to organize final findings in an interactive ArcGIS StoryMap, making the analysis accessible to local policymakers, planners, and community members.

TrailTell Genesee

A community-powered concept for park stewardship and land management.

UM-Flint Zillion Solutions competition
Top 10 finalist

View the public proposal ↗

Genesee County’s park system spans more than 11,000 acres, making it difficult for park staff and researchers to monitor every trail, invasive species outbreak, or maintenance concern. TrailTell Genesee is a conceptual proposal designed to connect park visitors with conservation work.

My work focused on system architecture, pitch deck design, and consultations with professors and industry professionals to make the idea practical for public land management. The proposal included two connected experiences:

  • Community app: Visitors could identify local plants and animals, collect field-journal observations, and submit geotagged hazard reports.
  • Ranger and researcher dashboard: A shared workspace could group duplicate reports, flag sensitive wildlife areas, and map invasive species observations over time.

To support long-term viability, I developed a launch strategy involving conservation grants, trailhead QR codes, and local business map sponsorships. The project taught me to translate complex ecological and spatial data needs into an accessible, community-centered proposal.
